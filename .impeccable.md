## Design Context

### Users
Mixed music students — some with prior training, some beginners. Using the app to practice note names, pitch identification, and music theory fundamentals with MIDI keyboard or mouse. Context: focused practice session, typically seated at a keyboard. Job: complete exercises, see progress, feel capable and not overwhelmed.

### Brand Personality
Warm · Encouraging · Scholarly

Emotional goal: students should feel **encouraged** — capable and making progress, not intimidated by music theory. The interface should feel like a good teacher: clear, patient, never patronising.

Reference: **Artusi** (artusimusic.com) — dark-themed, Architects Daughter + Montserrat fonts, neutral palette, compact 4px grid, teal `#2a7e94` primary, expressive motion. Students are familiar with this aesthetic from prior use.

Anti-reference: current student UI — `system-ui` font, flat blue `#4A90D9`, generic Bootstrap feel. Too bland, too big (viewport dominated by staff/piano with no room to breathe).

### Aesthetic Direction
**Light mode** (requested). Adapted Artusi design language to light: warm off-white backgrounds, teal `#2a7e94` as primary, Architects Daughter + Montserrat typography, 4px grid, subtle shadow elevation. Not a dark clone — genuinely warm and legible in light. Staff/piano area should be compact enough that students see the score **and** keyboard without scrolling.

### Design Principles
1. **Compact exercise view** — score and piano keyboard visible together without scrolling; avoid oversized margins
2. **Artusi typographic language** — Architects Daughter for UI labels/headings, Montserrat for body text
3. **Teal as the single accent** — `#2a7e94` for progress, active states, primary buttons; nothing else competes
4. **Progress is visible** — skill progress and exercise position always surfaced; students know where they stand
5. **Encouragement over instruction** — microcopy is warm and direct; no jargon, no patronising scaffolding text
